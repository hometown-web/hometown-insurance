<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Halloween is a time for spooky thrills and imagination. But the holiday also brings some extra risks for children and teens. Help keep Halloween fun and safe for your family with these tips.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Safety on the Trick-Or-Treat Trail</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>• <strong>Always accompany young children</strong> on their neighborhood rounds. If trick-or-treating doesn't start until after dark where you live, consider checking with your town or park district for Halloween activities offered earlier in the day.<br>• <strong>If your older children are trick-or-treating alone</strong>, plan and review the route that is acceptable to you. Agree on a specific time when they should return home and get flashlights with batteries for everyone.<br>• <strong>Talk with kids about the risk of distracted walking.</strong> This includes text messaging, talking on or looking at their cell phone and listening to music.<br>• <strong>Cross the street as a group in established crosswalks.</strong> Most child pedestrian traffic fatalities occurred mid-block, rather than at intersections. Make sure kids know not to cross the street between parked cars or out of driveways or alleys.<br>• <strong>Don't assume cars will stop</strong> just because they have the right of way. Motorists may have trouble seeing trick-or-treaters.<br>• <strong>Stay on well-lit streets and always use the sidewalk.</strong> If no sidewalk is available, walk at the far edge of the roadway facing traffic. Only go to homes with a porch light on and, ideally, a well-lit pathway.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Preparing Your Home for Trick-Or-Treaters</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>• <strong>Remove tripping hazards </strong>to keep your home safe for visiting trick-or-treaters. Keep the porch and front yard clear of items such as garden hoses, toys and bikes.<br>• <strong>Check outdoor lights</strong> and replace burned-out bulbs.<br>• <strong>Sweep wet leaves from sidewalks and steps</strong> to prevent anyone from slipping on them.<br>• <strong>Restrain pets</strong> so they do not jump on or bite a trick-or-treater.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Costume Safety Tips</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>• <strong>Plan costumes that are bright and reflective.</strong> A brightly colored treat bag or glow-in-the-dark elements can be a fun way to incorporate safety into a scary costume. Consider adding reflective tape or striping to costumes and trick-or-treat bags for greater visibility.<br>• <strong>Make sure that shoes fit well</strong>, and that costumes are short enough to prevent tripping, getting caught on objects or coming into contact with firepits.<br>• <strong>Look for "flame resistant" on the costume labels.</strong> Wigs and accessories should also clearly indicate this.<br>• <strong>Hats should fit properly</strong> to prevent them from sliding over eyes and blocking vision.<br>• <strong>Consider non-toxic makeup and decorative hats as safer alternatives to masks.</strong> Test makeup ahead of time on a small patch of your child's skin to ensure there are no unpleasant surprises or allergic reactions on the big day. Toxic ingredients have been found in cosmetics marketed to teens and tweens.<br>• <strong>Avoid any sharp or long swords, canes or sticks as a costume accessory. </strong>Your child can easily be hurt by these accessories if he or she stumbles or trips.<br>• <strong>Do not use decorative contact lenses without an eye exam and a prescription</strong> from an eye care professional. While packaging for decorative lenses may claim "<em>one size fits all</em>," or "<em>no need to see an eye specialist</em>," wearing decorative contact lenses without a prescription is dangerous. It can cause serious eye problems that can lead to permanent vision loss.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Pumpkins &amp; Decorating Safety</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>• <strong>Never allow small children to carve pumpkins.</strong> Children can draw a face with markers. Parents can do the cutting and then let little ones clean out the inside pulp and seeds. The <a href="https://www.assh.org/handcare/safety/pumpkin-carving" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">American Society for Surgery of the Hand</a> offers safe pumpkin carving tips.<br>• <strong>Consider using a battery-operated flameless candle, flashlight or glow stick instead</strong> of a candle to light your jack-o'-lantern. If you do use a candle, a votive candle is safest.<br>• <strong>Do not place candlelit pumpkins on a porch</strong> or any path where visitors may pass close by. They should never be left unattended.</p>
