With Thanksgiving and the holiday season right around the corner, it’s easy to get caught up in the chaos the coming weeks may bring. Between parties, gift shopping, and the cold weather, it’s easy to feel overwhelmed! One way to ease the chaos is to practice gratitude each day. Every day for the month of November, think about something you are thankful for, somebody you appreciate, or an experience that somehow improved your day—however small that experience may be. Track your gratitude personally in a journal, or share it with your friends via social media. Each November, Twitter and Instagram are flooded with posts featuring the hashtag “#30daysofthanks”, where users can share the little things in life that they’re thankful for.

Up for a challenge? Here are thirty thoughtful things you can do in November to show others your love, appreciation, and gratitude:
<ol>
 	<li>Do a secret act of appreciation for a family member</li>
 	<li>Have a low-tech evening with the family, playing board games or reading without cell phones, laptops, or the television to distract you</li>
 	<li>Buy coffee or food for the person behind you in line</li>
 	<li>Leave a generous tip</li>
 	<li>Give a neighbor a helping hand by raking leaves or shoveling snow from their driveway</li>
 	<li>Perform a random act of kindness</li>
 	<li>Volunteer at a homeless shelter or food bank</li>
 	<li>Donate clothing, furniture, household items, and toys to your local thrift store</li>
 	<li>Drive mindfully and carefully</li>
 	<li>Give others the benefit of the doubt</li>
 	<li>Write a note to a solider overseas, or a veteran in your area</li>
 	<li>Return a borrowed item you’ve been using</li>
 	<li>Hold the door for a stranger</li>
 	<li>Write thank-you notes for family members and friends</li>
 	<li>Make someone their favorite meal or a baked treat</li>
 	<li>Go out of your way to help a stranger</li>
 	<li>Feed someone else’s parking meter</li>
 	<li>Send a care package to someone far away</li>
 	<li>Give a stranger a genuine compliment</li>
 	<li>Bring some flowers into work to make the place more cheerful and inviting</li>
 	<li>Call a grandparent or loved one just to say hello</li>
 	<li>Have your child or grandchild write a note to thank their teacher</li>
 	<li>Have everyone at Thanksgiving dinner write down what they are thankful for this year, and what they need to learn to be thankful for. Show the answers at next year’s Thanksgiving—this can become a fun family tradition</li>
 	<li>Leave your postal worker a surprise in the mailbox: a gift card, a letter or some homemade cookies</li>
 	<li>Perform an act of kindness for your significant other: fix the leaky faucet, load and unload the dishwasher, do all the laundry for the day, mow the lawn</li>
 	<li>Spend the day together as a family</li>
 	<li>Watch a movie at home together as a family</li>
 	<li>Try something new: a food, drink, activity</li>
 	<li>Make holiday decorations together as a family</li>
 	<li>Decide on goals for December: do you want to continue the 30 days of gratitude? Maybe you’ll decide to not use your phone as much at home or to cook family dinners every night</li>
</ol>
