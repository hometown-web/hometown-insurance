<p>Risk assessment is fundamental to developing a realistic, reliable <a href="https://www.travelers.com/resources/business-topics/business-continuity/business-continuity-planning-in-4-steps">business continuity plan</a>. Companies that proactively consider which events are most likely to occur can focus disaster response planning efforts where they will yield the best return on investment – and remain better positioned to recover from a disaster.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Always base response and recovery strategies on an understanding of the threats your company faces, as well as their potential impact on business operations.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Most Common Threats</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The following list contains some common threats that may leave critical business resources and operations vulnerable.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>• <strong>Natural disasters</strong> such as tornadoes, hurricanes, floods, earthquakes, lightning strikes and wildfires.</p>
<p>• <strong>Manmade or technological events </strong>like fires and explosions, industrial accidents, chemical/hazardous material spills, communications and utility outages, system disruptions and transportation accidents.</p>
<p>• <strong>Malicious attacks </strong>including terrorism, bomb threats, vandalism, threats to reputation (off- or online), protests, civil unrest/riots, robbery and armed intruders.</p>
<p>• <strong>Cyberattacks </strong>such as denial of service attacks, computer viruses, worms, Trojan horses, cyberwarfare and cyberterrorism.</p>
<p>• <strong>Loss of workforce </strong>events such as long-term disability or illness, epidemic (e.g., flu, virus outbreaks), fatalities and worker strike.</p>
<p>• <strong>Supply chain disruptions</strong>, which can include counterfeit parts, regulatory requirement violations and transportation disruptions.</p>
<p>• <strong>Human error</strong>, such as poor training, poor maintenance, carelessness, misconduct, substance abuse, fatigue and counterfeit parts.</p>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Assessing Risk</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Although natural disasters seem to be happening more frequently than ever, particularly in the U.S., where nine of the 10 most costly disasters in 2012 occurred, * many business losses are actually caused by small events that are not widespread.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When determining what the biggest risks are for your businesses, consider the following:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>• <strong>Historical </strong>– What has happened in your community, to your facility or neighborhood before?</p>
<p>• <strong>Geographic </strong>– What is your proximity to flood plains, major airports, etc.? </p>
<p>• <strong>Physical </strong>– What is it about the design or construction of your facility/office that might make your business particularly susceptible to a certain event?</p>
<p>• <strong>Organizational </strong>– What is it about your employee, operational or technological infrastructure that might make your business particularly susceptible to a certain event?</p>
<p>• <strong>Regulatory </strong>– Is your business/industry required or mandated to prepare for any hazards?</p>
