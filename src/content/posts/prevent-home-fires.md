<p>Home fires can be preventable. The following are simple steps that each of us can take to prevent a tragedy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Cooking</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>• Stay in the kitchen when you are frying, grilling, or broiling food. Turn off the stove if you leave the kitchen for even a short period of time.</p>
<p>• Wear short, close-fitting, or tightly rolled sleeves when cooking.</p>
<p>• Position barbecue grills at least 10 feet away from siding and deck railings, and out from under eaves and overhanging branches.</p>
<p>• Keep a fire extinguisher available.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:list --></p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>Electrical and Appliance Safety</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>• Frayed wires can cause fires. Replace all worn, old or damaged appliance cords immediately and do not run cords under rugs or furniture.</p>
<p>• If an appliance has a three-prong plug, use it only in a three-slot outlet. Never force it to fit into a two-slot outlet or extension cord.</p>
<p>• Immediately shut off, then professionally replace, light switches that are hot to the touch and lights that flicker.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list --></p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>Keeping Children Safe</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>• Take the mystery out of fire play by teaching children that fire is a tool, not a toy.</p>
<p>• Store matches and lighters out of children's reach and sight, preferably in a locked cabinet.</p>
<p>• Never leave children unattended near operating stoves or burning candles, even for a short time.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list --></p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>Fireplaces and Woodstoves</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>• Inspect and clean woodstove pipes and chimneys annually and check monthly for damage or obstructions.</p>
<p>• Use a fireplace screen heavy enough to stop rolling logs and big enough to cover the entire opening of the fireplace to catch flying sparks.</p>
<p>• Make sure the fire is completely out before leaving the house or going to bed.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list --></p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>Portable Space Heaters</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>• Keep combustible objects at least three feet away from portable heating devices.</p>
<p>• Only buy heaters evaluated by a nationally recognized laboratory, such as Underwriters Laboratories (UL).</p>
<p>• Check to make sure the portable heater has a thermostat control mechanism and will switch off automatically if the heater falls over.</p>
<p>• Only use crystal clear K-1 kerosene in kerosene heaters. Never overfill it. Use the heater in a well-ventilated room away from curtains and other flammable items.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list --></p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>More Fire Prevention Tips</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>• Never use a stove range or oven to heat your home.</p>
<p>• Keep combustible and flammable liquids away from heat sources.</p>
<p>• Portable generators should NEVER be used indoors and should only be refueled outdoors and in well-ventilated areas.</p>
