<p>According to the National Highway Transportation Safety Association, the total economic impact of motor vehicle accidents was $340 billion in 2019, the equivalent of approximately $1,035 for every person living in the United States.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Here’s a look at key findings from the 2023 Travelers Risk Index and how a distracted driving policy can help businesses.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>2023 Risk Index for Business</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>WHAT’S DRIVING WORKERS TO DISTRACTION?</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>• A GROWING CONCERN FOR BUSINESSES</p>
<p>32% of business leaders are concerned about their employees using technology while driving for work purposes, up from 26% last year.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>• AND FOR GOOD REASON</p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>31% of employees admit to getting into a crash or collision while driving for work because they’re distracted, up from 26% last year.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>• DISTRACTED DRIVING EXCUSES</p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>44% of employees who take a work-related call while driving do so because it might be an emergency. 43% say they believe they always need to be available for work.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>• EMPLOYER EXPECTATIONS</p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>87% of employers expect their employees to respond to work-related messages when they are out of the office during work hours.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>HERE’S WHAT EMPLOYERS CAN DO:</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>• Have a policy: 74% of businesses have an official policy about employees sending or receiving phone calls, texts or emails while driving for work purposes. 60% of those require employees to sign a distracted driving contract or agreement.</p>
<p>• Train: 87% of businesses formally train employees on their distracted driving policy.</p>
<p>• Reinforce: 72% of companies discipline employees who do not comply with their policy while driving for work purposes.</p>
<p>• Monitor: 43% of companies use technology to track employees when they are driving.</p>
<p>• Speak up: Only 10% of people say they’ve spoken up to a colleague who was driving distracted. 84% of consumers who use a phone while driving say they would be less likely to if a passenger asked them to stop.</p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>TOP DISTRACTED DRIVING POLICY RULES:</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>• Do not use a handheld device while driving.</p>
<p>• Check directions or set your GPS before driving.</p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>The 2023 Risk Index finds employees are using their phone when they are behind the wheel, and it may contribute to distracted driving behaviors.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>In fact, the majority of employers (87%) expect their employees to respond to work-related messages when they are out of the office during work hours. And 44% of employees who take work-related calls while driving do so because they believe they always need to be available for work.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Business managers can help put a stop to this dangerous behavior by communicating and reinforcing driving policies, speaking up when a colleague is driving distracted and not calling employees when they know they are behind the wheel.</p>
