<p>Home prices continue to rise, but the dream of homeownership remains strong—and for good reason. Owning a home can be a powerful way to build wealth and long-term stability. Still, buying a house is a major financial and emotional commitment, so it’s important to be sure you’re truly ready before taking the leap.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Am I ready to buy a house?</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Before you start packing boxes, consider whether these pieces are in place:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>You have good credit.</strong> Credit scores play a big role in mortgage approval and interest rates. Generally, higher scores mean lower monthly payments.<br></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Your debt is manageable.</strong> You don’t need to be debt-free, but your total housing costs should ideally stay under 28% of your gross income, with total debt under 36%.<br></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>You’ve saved for a down payment.</strong> A 20% down payment can lower your monthly costs, though some loans allow less. Keep in mind that putting less than 20% down may require private mortgage insurance (PMI).<br></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>You can cover closing costs.</strong> In addition to your down payment, closing costs—such as inspections, appraisals, and title fees—often add 2%–5% to the purchase price.<br></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>You can afford ongoing monthly costs.</strong> Beyond the mortgage, factor in property taxes, homeowners insurance, utilities, HOA fees (if applicable), and maintenance.<br></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Your income is stable.</strong> Since a mortgage is a long-term commitment, reliable income is essential for long-term comfort and security.<br></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>If you’re ready to buy</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If many of these boxes are checked, here are a few smart next steps:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>- Determine how much house fits your budget.<br>- Compare mortgage rates and fees from multiple lenders.<br>- Get a pre-approval letter to strengthen your offer.<br>- Choose a knowledgeable real estate agent.<br>- Stick to your budget as you begin shopping.<br></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Not quite there yet?</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you’re not ready today, that’s okay. You can still take steps to prepare:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>- Improve your credit by paying down debt and making on-time payments.<br>- Build your down payment with consistent savings.<br>- Create a realistic budget.<br>- Set aside emergency savings for unexpected home repairs.<br></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"align":"left"} -->
<p class="has-text-align-left">Homeownership can offer stability, equity, and long-term benefits—but only when the timing is right. Taking the time to prepare now can help ensure a smoother, more confident transition when you’re ready to buy.<br></p>
