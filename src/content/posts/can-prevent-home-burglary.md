To best prevent burglary at your home, you should make your home unappealing to a burglar. The Burglary Prevention Council (BPC) suggests performing a security inspection on your home to determine how easy it would be for a burglar to get in. While completing this inspection be sure to check for the following:
<ul>
	<li>Consider installing a burglar alarm if your home does not already have one. Some insurance companies provide a 10 percent to 12 percent discount for a Central Monitored Alarm system to make a home safer.</li>
	<li>Trim any tall trees or hedges that can be easily climbed to gain access to a second story window</li>
	<li>Do not hide a spare key in an obvious location such as under a planter or doormat. Think about giving the spare key to a neighbor instead.</li>
	<li>Do you have outdoor lighting (street light, porch light)? Burglars are much more likely to break into a dark yard where they can easily hide.</li>
	<li>Make sure you have heavy doors and windows that are equipped with strong, functional locks.</li>
	<li>Rearrange your home furnishings so that your valuables are not easily seen through windows and doors.</li>
	<li>If currently using a door chain to screen visitors, consider using a peep hole that will prevent you from having to open the door to identify visitors.</li>
</ul>
<b>Try practicing the following good habits for preventing break-ins:</b>
<ol start="1">
	<li>Follow a routine to ensure that doors and windows are locked and security systems are activated at night.</li>
	<li>Notify the police if you see suspicious activity/persons in your neighborhood.</li>
	<li>Do not carry your house key with a key ring, or in a wallet that bears your address.</li>
</ol>
